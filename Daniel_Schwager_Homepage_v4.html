§ 02

The Approach

Treatment begins with
understanding the whole picture.

Persistent pain is rarely about the place it hurts. Daniel's work starts by looking further — at movement, muscular balance, past injury, physical history, and the small compensations the body has quietly made along the way.

Each session is built around the person in front of him. Manual therapy, acupuncture and dry needling are used in combination or in isolation, depending on what the body is asking for that week. The intention is straightforward: to help the underlying pattern resolve, rather than to manage the symptom in circles.

It is a slower, more considered form of practice. There are no packages, no protocols, and no one-size treatment plans. Treatment is a conversation between what Daniel finds and what the patient reports — refined session by session.

§ 04

Treatments

Three disciplines,
one considered practice.

The tools are chosen to suit the person, never the other way around. Sessions may draw on one method or several — and change direction as the picture becomes clearer.

i.

Manual Therapy

Hands-on treatment of soft tissue, joints and movement — the foundation of Daniel's practice, developed over three decades. Deep tissue and myofascial work, remedial soft tissue therapy, joint mobilisation, and postural assessment.

Used to release restricted tissue, improve movement, and let the body find its own way back to a more efficient pattern.

ii.

Acupuncture

Studied in the Western medical tradition and drawn on where it is likely to make a meaningful difference — for pain modulation, muscular tension, and nervous system regulation.

Used as part of a broader treatment approach rather than as a stand-alone therapy. Where the picture calls for it, it is often the most effective route in.

iii.

Dry Needling

A precise, targeted technique for trigger points, restricted tissue and referred pain. Not the same as acupuncture — a Western-medical tool for very specific clinical situations.

Used sparingly and deliberately, when the tissue is asking for that particular kind of attention.

How a session unfolds.

i.

Conversation

History, symptoms, and what has been tried. Daniel listens first.

ii.

Assessment

Movement, palpation, and reading how the body is compensating.

iii.

Treatment

Chosen combination of manual work, acupuncture or dry needling.

iv.

Follow-through

A brief note on what to notice, and — if useful — what to work on between sessions.

About Daniel

Experience matters.
So does listening.

Daniel Schwager is a Swiss-born, London-based Manual Therapist and Acupuncturist. He began practising manual and soft tissue therapy in 1994 and trained in acupuncture in 2007 — bringing the two disciplines together into a single, integrated way of working.

His knowledge is not only clinical. He draws on anatomy, movement, sport, yoga and Pilates — the study of how bodies are put together, and how they hold themselves under load. What arrives in the room is the sum of all of it: a practitioner, not a technician; a diagnostician as much as a therapist.

Above all, his hands are his most valuable instrument — sensitive, experienced and precise. Three decades of tactile listening are what a first visit is really made of.
